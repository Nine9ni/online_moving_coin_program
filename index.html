You Are Being Asked to Be in a Research Study

Concise presentation of key concepts

You are being asked to be in a research study. A research study is designed to answer a scientific question. If you agree to be in the study, you will be one of 300 people above the age of 18 who are being studied at Emory. The study sessions lasts between 15 and 30 minutes.

Why is this study being done?

This study is being done to see whether visual imagery can be assessed using pupillometry, which is measurement of how your pupils expand and contract. In this study, we measure your pupils’ reactions to visual objects when directly observable or when you are asked to imagine them. The study may ultimately be used to test the development of imagery in even young children and among individuals who report not being able to engage in this important cognitive ability.

Do you have to be in the study?

It is your choice to join this research study. You do not have to be in it. Before you decide, take the time to learn about the study.

What do you have to do if you choose to join this study?

If you qualify and choose to join the study, you will sit in front of a computer monitor with you heading resting in a chinrest to avoid excessive movements. You will observe visual objects while they are onscreen and you will also be asked to imagine the objects when they are no longer present.

How is this study going to help you?

If you are in the study, you will be helping the researchers answer the study question. This study is not intended to benefit you directly.

What are the risks or discomforts you should know about before deciding?

This study will take time to complete. All studies have some risks. The risks in this study are minimal:

loss of time

boredom

Alternatives to Joining This Study

The alternative to participating in this study is to write a research report on a published study provided by the instructor(s) of PSYC110/111.

Costs

There will be no costs to you for participating in this study, other than transportation costs if you are not already at Emory. You will not be charged for any of the research activities.

What Should You Do Next?

Please read this form, or have it read to you. Make sure the experimenter explains the study to you. Ask questions such as how much time you will have to spend on the study, any words you do not understand, and more details about study procedures.

What is the purpose of this study?

The purpose of this study is for us to see whether visual imagery can be detected by measuring your pupils’ responses. Pupils respond to light—for example, they constrict under bright light and dilate under dark light. In this study, we examine whether pupils respond in a similar way when only imagining light and dark objects. This study will help us learn about visual imagery and may ultimately be used to test the development of imagery in even young children, and among individuals who report not being able to engage in this important cognitive ability.

What will you be asked to do?

As a participant in this study, you will be asked to view different images as well as to imagine those images at a subsequent time.

Who owns your study data and samples?

If you join this study, you will be donating your data. You will not be paid if your data are used to make a new product. If you decide to leave the study prior to completion, the data that was already collected may still be used for this study, unless you request that it be destroyed. If you decide to withdraw from the study, you have the right to request that we destroy, or not use, the data already collected.

What are the possible risks and discomforts?

The potential risks of participating in this study are minimal and consist primarily of loss of time and/or boredom from the number of trials required. The stimuli consist of light and dark images. In rare cases, changes in brightness may cause dizziness or a migraine. Although all data are deidentified, there is a small risk of loss of confidentiality.

Will you benefit from the study?

The results of this study will contribute to our understanding of visual imagery. If you are interested in the specifics of the study, we will be happy to tell you more about our project. However, there are no direct benefits to you for participation in this study. The study results may be used to help others in the future.

Will you be paid for your time and effort?

Participants will receive 1 participation credit applied to the course.

How will your private information be protected?

Data will be stored securely on password protected servers. Data will only be made available to persons conducting the study. However, agencies that make rules and policy about how research is done have the right to review these records. This includes the Office for Human Research Protections, and the Emory University Institutional Review Board. No references to the study will contain any identifying information that could link you to any particular findings, or even to your participation.

Certificate of Confidentiality

The Certificate of Confidentiality helps us to keep others from learning that you participated in this study. Emory will rely on the Certificate of Confidentiality to refuse to give out study information that identifies you. For example, if Emory received a subpoena for study records, it would not give out information that identifies you.

The Certificate of Confidentiality does not stop you or someone else, like a member of your family, from giving out information about your participation in this study. For example, if you let your insurance company know that you are in this study, and you agree to give the insurance company research information, then the investigator cannot use the Certificate to withhold this information. This means you and your family also need to protect your own privacy.

The Certificate does not stop Emory from making the following disclosures about you:

Giving state public health officials information about certain infectious diseases,

Giving law officials information about abuse of a child, elderly person or disabled person.

Giving out information to prevent harm to you or others.

Giving the study sponsor or funders information about the study, including information for an audit or evaluation.

Storing and Sharing your Information

We will store all the data that you provide using a code. We need this code so that we can keep track of your data over time. This code will not include information that can identify you (identifiers). Specifically, it will not include your name, initials, or date of birth. We will keep a file that links this code to your identifiers in a secure location separate from the data.

We will not allow your name and any other fact that might point to you to appear when we present or publish the results of this study.

Your data may be useful for other research being done by investigators at Emory or elsewhere. We may share the data, linked by the study code, with other researchers at Emory, or with researchers at other institutions that maintain at least the same level of data security that we maintain at Emory. We will not share the link between the study code and your identity.

We may also place data in public databases accessible to researchers who agree to maintain data confidentiality, but only if the study code is removed and we can ensure that the data are anonymized to a level that it is highly unlikely that anyone could identify you.
